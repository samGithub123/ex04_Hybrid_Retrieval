Overview
A Loan Against Property (LAP) is a secured loan where you pledge an owned property (residential or commercial, subject to policy) as collateral to borrow funds for a wide range of needs. LAP is often used for business expansion, education funding, medical expenses, or consolidating high-cost debt, while benefiting from longer tenors than typical unsecured loans.

Why LAP can be useful
• Higher ticket sizes may be available because the loan is secured
• Longer repayment tenure can reduce monthly burden
• Potentially better pricing compared to unsecured loans (profile-dependent)
• Flexible end-use (within permissible and policy limits)

Common end-use examples
Business needs: working capital, expansion, equipment purchase, renovation of business premises.
Education: higher studies, professional courses, overseas education.
Medical: planned procedures and recovery needs.
Debt consolidation: closing multiple high-cost facilities into one structured EMI.

Collateral and property types
Accepted property types may include:
• Self-occupied residential property
• Rented residential property
• Commercial property (shops/offices), subject to evaluation
The property must have clear title and acceptable legal standing. We conduct legal and technical checks to validate the collateral.

Eligibility (high-level)
LAP eligibility typically includes:
• Ownership and clear title of the collateral property
• Satisfactory credit profile and repayment behavior
• Adequate income/cashflow to service the EMI
• Acceptable property valuation and location
• Compliance with policy on property age, usage, and documentation

Property evaluation and due diligence
1) Technical/valuation: assesses market value, condition, and liquidity of the property.
2) Legal check: verifies title chain, encumbrances, approvals, and any pending disputes.
These steps reduce risk and help ensure the loan is responsibly sanctioned.

Documents commonly required
Borrower KYC and income proof (similar to personal loan/home loan).
Bank statements to assess cashflow and commitments.
Property documents: title deed, chain documents, tax receipts, approved plan, completion/occupancy documents (where applicable), NOCs, and any existing loan details on the property.
Business documents (for self-employed): registration, GST returns, financial statements, etc. (if applicable).

Loan amount and tenor
Loan amounts depend on:
• Property value and policy-based LTV
• Borrower income and repayment capacity
• Existing encumbrances on the property (if any)
Tenor is typically longer than personal loans but varies by program and customer profile.

Pricing and fees
LAP pricing is based on:
• Credit profile and income stability
• Property type and liquidity
• Loan amount and tenor
Typical charges may include processing fee, valuation/legal fees, documentation charges, and charges for delays or mandate failures. Full details are provided in the KFS and agreement.

Repayment, prepayment, and foreclosure
• Repayments are usually via monthly EMIs through auto-debit.
• Partial prepayment can reduce interest burden and shorten tenor.
• Foreclosure/part-prepayment terms vary by policy and rate structure. Review your KFS/loan agreement for charges and conditions.

Important considerations before choosing LAP
• A secured loan means the collateral property is at risk if repayments are not maintained.
• Borrow only what you need and can comfortably repay.
• Ensure property documents are complete to avoid delays.
• Keep your EMI-to-income ratio conservative to manage business cycles.
