Overview
A Home Loan is a secured loan used for purchasing or constructing a residential property, and in some programs, for renovation or balance transfer. Because the loan is backed by the property, home loans generally offer longer tenors and may offer more favorable pricing than unsecured credit, subject to your profile and property evaluation.

What you can finance
• Purchase of a ready-to-move-in home
• Purchase of a resale property
• Construction on an owned plot (subject to approvals)
• Home improvement/renovation (select programs)
• Balance transfer (moving an existing home loan from another lender), subject to evaluation

Key features
• Longer tenors that can reduce EMI burden
• Structured repayment schedule with clear amortization
• Property-backed lending with legal and technical due diligence
• Options for salaried and self-employed applicants (program-dependent)
• Co-applicant support (e.g., spouse/parents) to strengthen eligibility

How eligibility is assessed
Eligibility typically depends on:
• Income and stability (salary continuity, business vintage, cashflow)
• Credit history and current obligations
• Age and repayment horizon
• Property characteristics (location, legal title, market value)
• Down payment capacity and overall affordability

Property checks (why they matter)
Home loans involve two important property checks:
1) Technical evaluation: on-site/desk valuation to estimate market value and condition.
2) Legal due diligence: verification of title chain, approvals, encumbrances, and compliance.
These checks protect both the borrower and the lender by reducing legal risks and ensuring the property can be financed.

Documents commonly requested
Borrower KYC: identity and address proof.
Income documents: salary slips, Form 16, ITRs, business financials (as applicable).
Bank statements: recent months for income and obligations.
Property documents: agreement to sell, title deed, chain documents, approved building plan, occupancy certificate/CC (where applicable), tax receipts, and NOC details.
Builder documents (for under-construction): project approvals, RERA registration (where applicable), and other compliance records.

Loan amount, down payment, and LTV
Home loans usually finance a portion of the property cost; the remaining is your down payment.
The financing ratio (often expressed as Loan-to-Value, LTV) depends on policy, property type, and risk assessment. Higher down payment can reduce EMI and total interest.

Interest rate structure
Home loans can be structured as:
• Fixed rate (rate remains constant for a defined period or full term, as per product)
• Floating/variable rate (rate can change with benchmark movements)
Your KFS and sanction letter will clearly indicate the rate type, benchmark (if any), and reset rules.

Fees and charges (typical components)
• Processing fee and administrative charges
• Valuation/technical assessment fee
• Legal due diligence fee
• Insurance (optional or bundled depending on program)
• Stamp duty/registration and government levies (paid to authorities, not a lender fee)

Disbursal types
• Full disbursal: for ready properties after documentation is complete.
• Part disbursal / stage-wise: for under-construction properties based on construction milestones and demand letters.

Repayment and prepayment
• EMIs are usually monthly through auto-debit.
• Partial prepayment may reduce your principal and overall interest.
• Foreclosure rules depend on loan type, rate structure, and policy; details are in your loan agreement and KFS.

Helpful guidance for home buyers
• Keep a buffer for registration, interiors, and moving costs beyond the down payment.
• Verify the property’s legal approvals early to avoid delays.
• Compare APR and total cost, not just the advertised rate.
• Maintain a stable repayment record; it matters for future credit.
